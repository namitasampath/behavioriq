All figures come from `python run_all.py` (seed 42, 6,000 simulated sessions, 74,513 events,
time-based 70/15/15 split). Every number below is reproducible; full tables are in `reports/`.

## Findings (write these honestly - they are a stronger viva than inflated numbers)

1. **Representation (RQ1).** A fine-tuned Transformer (C-ft, macro-F1 0.848) roughly matches
   hand-crafted sequence features (B, 0.861) and beats aggregate features (A, 0.843). The *frozen*
   contrastive embedding (C, 0.757) is weaker; self-supervised pretraining alone does not capture
   the outcome-relevant signal.
2. **Retrieval augmentation (RQ2).** Adding nearest-neighbour outcomes gives a small gain over C
   (+0.0065 macro-F1, +0.0028 ROC-AUC) that is not statistically significant (p = 0.18 and 0.08,
   paired bootstrap). On top of B it does not help (p = 0.89). Interpretation: when a session's
   own features already identify its outcome, neighbour evidence is redundant.
3. **Where retrieval helps (RQ3).** In early prefixes the learned/retrieval variants are strongest:
   after 3 events C (0.389) and B+R / C+R (0.386) lead A (0.370) and B (0.364); after 5 events B+R
   leads (0.592). The advantage disappears as more of the session is observed. This is the
   early-prediction curve figure (`reports/stage8/early_curve.png`).
4. **Decisioning (RQ4).** Thompson Sampling and LinUCB earn ~16% more reward than the static
   "discount if predicted abandon" rule and learn to favour comparison help over discounts. The
   static rule is worse than random, because it targets discounts at intent rather than at
   price sensitivity.
5. **Design lesson (encoder).** Including product category in the tokens made the contrastive
   encoder group sessions by category instead of behaviour (neighbour agreement 0.455 vs 0.577).
   End-anchored crops were also needed, because the most recent events carry intent.
6. **Drift.** Persona-mix shifts move each feature only slightly. Two of five were caught only by the
   multivariate domain-classifier test, not by per-feature PSI.

## Threats to validity (put in Limitations)
- Synthetic data generated by a first-order Markov process favours count/transition features;
  real sessions with longer-range structure may favour the Transformer. Next step: REES46 replay.
- The bandit is evaluated only in simulation (no counterfactuals in logged data).
- The text baseline is TF-IDF + SVD unless `sentence-transformers` is installed (it then uses MiniLM).
- The laptop edition substitutes in-process equivalents for Kafka/Flink/Feast/ClickHouse (README table).

## Likely viva questions
- *Why a time-based split?* Random splits let the model see the future and inflate scores.
- *How do you stop label leakage?* Inputs end before the first purchase/exit event (`prediction_prefix`).
- *How do you stop retrieval leakage?* Neighbours must close before the query session starts, and never include itself.
- *Why HDBSCAN?* Behavioural clusters are uneven and non-spherical, and it labels outliers as noise instead of forcing them into a cluster.
- *Why Thompson Sampling?* It balances exploration and exploitation by sampling from its uncertainty, and it beats fixed rules once it has some data.
- *How is the copilot kept honest?* SQL is limited to whitelisted views, rows from fewer than 20 sessions are suppressed, and every number in an answer is checked against tool output.
